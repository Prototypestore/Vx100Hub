Case Study

A breakdown of strategy, design decisions, and measurable outcomes.

Overview

This is my personal website. I built it to properly show my work, my style, and what I can actually do as a designer — not just throw things online and hope it looks good.

The goal was simple: clean, structured, and easy to understand the second someone lands on it.

It’s my main portfolio space, but it also reflects how I think about design in general — whether it’s websites or graphic design. It all comes down to structure, spacing, and how things communicate visually.

Problem

The website before this didn’t feel fully intentional.

It had content, but:
the structure wasn’t clear
the flow between sections felt off
there wasn’t a strong hierarchy
and it didn’t guide the viewer properly

So even if the work was there, it didn’t come across the way it should.

Approach

Style Analysis

I looked at modern portfolio and agency websites to understand what makes them feel clean, how they guide attention, how spacing affects perception, and why simple layouts usually look more professional. I didn’t copy anything — I just studied what actually works.

Visual Structure Rebuild

This was the main change. I rebuilt the layout so it actually flows: sections follow a clear order, spacing is more consistent, alignment is cleaner throughout, and unnecessary clutter was removed. The goal was clarity above everything else.

Typography Refinement

Typography was simplified and structured properly with clearer hierarchy, consistent font usage, better spacing between text sections, and easier scanability. Small changes, but a big difference in readability.

Inspiration-Based Reconstruction

I took inspiration from high-end portfolio and agency sites, but rebuilt everything in my own way. The focus was clean structure, strong hierarchy, and minimal but intentional design — not copying, just understanding what works and applying it properly.

Solution

The final website is a cleaner, more structured portfolio that communicates things properly.

Now it clearly shows what I do, feels more professional, is easier to navigate, and actually guides the viewer through the work.

It finally feels intentional, not random.

Outcome

The website now works as my portfolio, my personal design identity, and a clear entry point for potential clients.

It’s not just a display of work anymore — it communicates it properly.

The same approach carries into my graphic design work too, because whether it’s a website or a post, I always focus on structure first.

Tools Used

  • HTML5
  • CSS3
  • JavaScript

Final Note

This project is part of how I’m building my direction as a designer.

I’m focused on clean, structured, intentional work — design that actually communicates something, not just decoration.
